# geebeer
 GEE BEE R1 & R2 SUPER SPORTSTER for Aerofly FS4

The Gee Bee R1 and R2 Super Sportster were designed specifically for the 1932 Thompson and Bendix races. They were the fastest planes of their time, and certainly the most recognizable ones with their so particular shape. Constructed by the Granville brothers and engineer Howell W. "Pete" Miller, they earned a reputation of dangerous machines...

For those interested in learning more about the history of these particular
aircrafts and their builders, I recommend the following books and sites:

- 'THE GEE BEE RACERS' A Legacy of Speed
   by Charles A. Mendenhall & Tom Murphy - Specialty Press
   The story of the Granville Brothers and their machines

General considerations

- speed is in mph.
- Be sure to set the flight model per frame at 2 or more (Operations and Warnings menu).
- Plane must be trimmed before take-off due to massive engine torque.
- Don't apply full throttle on take-off, especially with R1 model.
- Side door opens with speedbrakes command.
- You can remove the windshield by pressing an invisible button above the artificial horizon.
- The interior, handles and panel are not 100% accurate (will occur in a future update... maybe).

Gee Bee R2 Super Sportster
1932 version NR2101 number 7

INTRODUCTION
The R2 is the sister ship of the R1. Except a few minor differences, they were identical. The R2 was intended to compete the Bendix Trophy, a cross country race from Los Angeles to Cleveland. Lee Gehlbach flew it and made fourth. Gehlbach also entered the Thompson Trophy and took the fifth place.

CHARACTERISTICS
Empty weight : 1796 lb
Gross weight : 3883 lb with full tanks - 2371 lb with 50 gallons of fuel for 
closed course racing
Powerplant : 550 horsepower Pratt & Whitney Wasp Jr. 985
Maximum speed : 250 mph
Tail wheel : fixed (can be unlatched for easier handling)
Propeller : Smith controllable pitch
Nav light : wing tip and tail fin

Gee Bee R1 Super Sportster
1933 version NR2100 number 11

INTRODUCTION
In 1933 the R1 is re-engined with a more powerful engine. To counteract the increased torque, a 10 inch rudder extension is added at the lower end. Participating to the Bendix Trophy this year, the plane crashed on take-off in Indianapolis. Pilot Russell Boardman died two days later.

INTRODUCTION
In 1933 the R2 is re-engined with the R1's previous engine. Rudder extension is added in the same way that for the R1. New wings are thicker and have an increased area. Flaps are added too, lowering the landing speed at 65 mph.

The 1932 R-2 was identical to the 1932 R-1 except that it used a smaller 550 hp (410 kW) Pratt & Whitney Wasp Junior (R-985) nine cylinder radial powerplant, with a narrower engine cowling, as the aircraft was intended primarily as a cross-country racer with a larger fuel capacity of 302 US gal (1,140 L; 251 imp gal) to increase the distance between fuel stops. The gross weight of the R-2 with full tanks was 3,883 lb (1,761 kg). In 1933, the R-2 was modified with the more powerful Pratt & Whitney Wasp and its cowling from the 1932 R-1 which was uprated for 1933 with the bigger, more powerful Pratt & Whitney R-1690 Hornet. Other 1933 R-2 modifications included a new thicker wing with a longer span of 27.8 ft (8.5 m) and an area of 104 sq ft (9.7 m2), and Granville's 2-piece, double hinged flaps to aid in getting in and out of very short runways with a full fuel load. The landing speed of the R-2 was cut from 100 to 65 mph (161 to 105 km/h). Both racers also got an aluminum extension to their rudder.[17][18]
